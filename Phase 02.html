Module 01 • Geometry

Unit 2.1: Tangent Lines and Instantaneous Velocity

The Intuitive Real-Life Hook: Speedometers and Slopes

If you drive 120 miles in 2 hours, your average velocity is 60 mph. But what does your speedometer read at exactly 1 hour and 15 minutes? That is instantaneous velocity. In calculus, average velocity corresponds to the slope of a secant line connecting two points on a position-time graph. As those two points merge into one, the secant line becomes a tangent line, and its slope represents the exact instantaneous rate of change at that moment [cite: 3, 4].

The Mathematical Engine: Definitions

Tangent Line Definition [cite: 3]

The tangent line to the curve $y=f(x)$ at the point $P(a, f(a))$ is the line through $P$ with slope $m = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$, provided this limit exists.

Instantaneous Velocity

If $s = f(t)$ is the position function of a particle, its instantaneous velocity at time $t$ is the derivative $v(t) = s'(t) = \frac{ds}{dt}$ [cite: 4].

ISRT 1st Year 2nd In-course Exam 2024 Problem 1 & 2

Question:

1. Find an equation for the tangent line to the curve $y = 2/x$ at the point $(2,1)$ on this curve [cite: 4].

2. Consider a particle whose position function is $s = f(t) = 1 + 5t - 2t^2$. The position of the particle at time $t = 2$ s is $s = 3$ m. Find the particle's instantaneous velocity at time $t = 2$ s [cite: 4].

Model Exam Solution (Written for Full Marks):
Part 1: Tangent Line Equation

Given $y = f(x) = \frac{2}{x} = 2x^{-1}$. We need the slope at $x = 2$.

$$f'(x) = -2x^{-2} = -\frac{2}{x^2}$$

Evaluate the slope $m$ at $x=2$:

$$m = f'(2) = -\frac{2}{(2)^2} = -\frac{2}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$$

Using the point-slope form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ with point $(2, 1)$:

$$y - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 2)$$
$$y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 1 + 1 \implies y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 2$$
Part 2: Instantaneous Velocity

The position function is $s(t) = 1 + 5t - 2t^2$.

The velocity function $v(t)$ is the derivative of position with respect to time:

$$v(t) = s'(t) = \frac{d}{dt}(1 + 5t - 2t^2) = 5 - 4t$$

Evaluate the velocity at $t = 2$ s:

$$v(2) = 5 - 4(2) = 5 - 8 = -3 \text{ m/s}$$
Module 02 • Advanced Differentiation

Unit 2.2: Implicit Differentiation and the Folium of Descartes

The Intuitive Real-Life Hook: Curves that loop

Most functions we see are explicitly solved for $y$, like $y = x^2$. But many beautiful and important geometric curves, like circles ($x^2 + y^2 = r^2$) or the Folium of Descartes ($x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$), twist and cross themselves, failing the vertical line test [cite: 3, 4]. We cannot isolate $y$ easily. Implicit differentiation allows us to find the slope $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at any point on these complex curves by differentiating both sides with respect to $x$ and applying the chain rule whenever we differentiate a term containing $y$ [cite: 3, 4].

Examiner Trap & Memory Trick: The Missing Chain Rule

When differentiating $y^3$ implicitly with respect to $x$, students often write $3y^2$ and stop. This is wrong! Because $y$ is a function of $x$, the chain rule requires multiplying by the inner derivative: $\frac{d}{dx}(y^3) = 3y^2 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$. Similarly, terms like $xy$ require the product rule: $\frac{d}{dx}(xy) = 1 \cdot y + x \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$ [cite: 3, 4].

ISRT Final Examination 2024 Problem 4

Question:

(a) Use implicit differentiation to find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for the Folium of Descartes: $x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$ [cite: 3, 4].

(b) Find an equation for the tangent line to the Folium of Descartes at the point $(\frac{3}{2}, \frac{3}{2})$ [cite: 3, 4].

Model Exam Solution (Written for Full Marks):
Part (a): Implicit Differentiation

Given $x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, using the chain rule on $y^3$ and the product rule on $3xy$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^3) + \frac{d}{dx}(y^3) = \frac{d}{dx}(3xy)$$
$$3x^2 + 3y^2 \frac{dy}{dx} = 3 \left( y + x\frac{dy}{dx} \right)$$

Divide the entire equation by 3 to simplify:

$$x^2 + y^2 \frac{dy}{dx} = y + x\frac{dy}{dx}$$

Group all terms with $\frac{dy}{dx}$ on one side:

$$y^2 \frac{dy}{dx} - x\frac{dy}{dx} = y - x^2$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} (y^2 - x) = y - x^2$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y - x^2}{y^2 - x}$$
Part (b): Tangent Line Equation

Evaluate the derivative at the point $(x, y) = (\frac{3}{2}, \frac{3}{2})$ to find the slope $m$:

$$m = \frac{\frac{3}{2} - (\frac{3}{2})^2}{(\frac{3}{2})^2 - \frac{3}{2}} = \frac{\frac{3}{2} - \frac{9}{4}}{\frac{9}{4} - \frac{3}{2}}$$
$$m = \frac{\frac{6}{4} - \frac{9}{4}}{\frac{9}{4} - \frac{6}{4}} = \frac{-\frac{3}{4}}{\frac{3}{4}} = -1$$

Using the point-slope form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$:

$$y - \frac{3}{2} = -1 \left(x - \frac{3}{2}\right)$$
$$y = -x + \frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{2} \implies y = -x + 3 \implies x + y = 3$$
Module 03 • Dynamic Systems

Unit 2.3: Related Rates

The Intuitive Real-Life Hook: Connected Movements

When variables are tied together by geometry (like the sides of a right triangle via the Pythagorean theorem), their rates of change are also tied together [cite: 4]. If a baseball player runs from second to third base, his distance to third base is shrinking at a known rate. But how fast is his distance from home plate changing? Because the diamond is a square, these distances form a right triangle. By taking the derivative of the Pythagorean theorem with respect to time ($t$), we link the unknown rate to the known rates [cite: 4].

ISRT 1st Year 2nd In-course Exam 2024 Problem 8

Question:

A baseball diamond is a square whose sides are 90 ft long. Suppose that a player running from second base to third base has a speed of 30 ft/s at the instant when he is 20 ft from third base. At what rate is the player's distance from home plate changing at that instant? [cite: 4]

Model Exam Solution (Written for Full Marks):

Let the baseball diamond be oriented on a coordinate plane with home plate at the origin $(0,0)$. First base is at $(90, 0)$, second base is at $(90, 90)$, and third base is at $(0, 90)$.

The player is running from second base $(90, 90)$ to third base $(0, 90)$. Therefore, the player's y-coordinate is constant at $y=90$, and their x-coordinate is decreasing.

Step 1: Define Variables and Given Information

  • Let $x$ be the player's distance from third base. Because the side length is 90 ft, the player's coordinate position is $(x, 90)$.
  • Let $z$ be the player's direct distance from home plate $(0,0)$.
  • We are given the rate of change of $x$: $\frac{dx}{dt} = -30 \text{ ft/s}$ (negative because the distance to third base is decreasing).
  • We need to find $\frac{dz}{dt}$ at the instant when $x = 20 \text{ ft}$.

Step 2: Establish the Geometric Relationship

Using the distance formula or Pythagorean theorem for the triangle formed by home plate, third base, and the player:

$$z^2 = x^2 + 90^2$$

Step 3: Differentiate with Respect to Time ($t$)

$$\frac{d}{dt}(z^2) = \frac{d}{dt}(x^2 + 8100)$$
$$2z \frac{dz}{dt} = 2x \frac{dx}{dt} \implies z \frac{dz}{dt} = x \frac{dx}{dt}$$

Step 4: Solve for Unknowns at the Given Instant

When $x = 20$, we must find the corresponding value of $z$:

$$z^2 = 20^2 + 90^2 = 400 + 8100 = 8500 \implies z = \sqrt{8500} = 10\sqrt{85}$$

Substitute all known values into the differentiated equation:

$$(10\sqrt{85}) \frac{dz}{dt} = (20)(-30)$$
$$(10\sqrt{85}) \frac{dz}{dt} = -600$$
$$\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{-600}{10\sqrt{85}} = -\frac{60}{\sqrt{85}} \approx -6.51 \text{ ft/s}$$

The player's distance from home plate is decreasing at a rate of $60/\sqrt{85}$ ft/s.

Module 04 • Optimization

Unit 2.4: Extrema and Optimization

The Mathematical Engine: Definitions

Absolute Maxima [cite: 3]

Let $f$ be a function defined on some interval $I$ containing $c$. Then $f(c)$ is the absolute maximum of $f$ on $I$ if $f(c) \ge f(x)$ for all $x$ in $I$ [cite: 3].

Relative Extrema [cite: 3]

A function $f$ has a relative maximum (or minimum) at $c$ if there exists an open interval $(a, b)$ containing $c$ such that $f(c) \ge f(x)$ (or $f(c) \le f(x)$) for all $x$ in $(a, b)$ [cite: 3].

Extreme Value Theorem [cite: 3]

If $f$ is continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$, then $f$ attains both an absolute maximum value $f(c)$ and an absolute minimum value $f(d)$ at some numbers $c$ and $d$ in $[a, b]$ [cite: 3].

ISRT 1st Year 2nd In-course Exam 2024 Problem 9

Question:

An open box is to be made from a 16-inch by 30-inch piece of cardboard by cutting out squares of equal size from the four corners and bending up the sides. What size should the squares be to obtain a box with the largest volume? [cite: 4]

Model Exam Solution (Written for Full Marks):

Step 1: Define Variables and the Objective Function

Let $x$ be the side length of the square cut from each corner. This $x$ will become the height of the folded box.

  • Length of the box base: $L = 30 - 2x$
  • Width of the box base: $W = 16 - 2x$
  • Height of the box: $H = x$

The volume of the box is $V(x) = L \cdot W \cdot H$:

$$V(x) = (30 - 2x)(16 - 2x)(x) = (480 - 60x - 32x + 4x^2)x$$
$$V(x) = 4x^3 - 92x^2 + 480x$$

The domain for $x$ is constrained by the shorter side of the cardboard. We cannot cut out more than half of 16 inches, so $0 < x < 8$.

Step 2: Find Critical Points

Differentiate $V(x)$ with respect to $x$ and set to zero:

$$V'(x) = 12x^2 - 184x + 480 = 0$$

Divide by 4 to simplify:

$$3x^2 - 46x + 120 = 0$$

Use the quadratic formula to solve for $x$:

$$x = \frac{-(-46) \pm \sqrt{(-46)^2 - 4(3)(120)}}{2(3)} = \frac{46 \pm \sqrt{2116 - 1440}}{6}$$
$$x = \frac{46 \pm \sqrt{676}}{6} = \frac{46 \pm 26}{6}$$

This gives two possible roots:

$$x_1 = \frac{72}{6} = 12 \quad \text{and} \quad x_2 = \frac{20}{6} = \frac{10}{3} = 3\frac{1}{3}$$

Step 3: Verification

The root $x = 12$ is outside our valid domain $(0, 8)$, as cutting a 12-inch square from a 16-inch side is impossible. Therefore, the only physically meaningful critical point is $x = 10/3$.

To confirm it is a maximum, check the second derivative:

$$V''(x) = 24x - 184$$
$$V''(10/3) = 24(10/3) - 184 = 80 - 184 = -104$$

Since $V''(10/3) < 0$, the function is concave down, confirming a local maximum.

$$\text{The squares should be } \frac{10}{3} \text{ inches by } \frac{10}{3} \text{ inches.}$$

Practice Problem 1 Implicit Differentiation [cite: 3, 4, 8]

Show that $x^3 + 3xy^2 = 1$ is an implicit solution to the differential equation $2xy \frac{dy}{dx} + x^2 + y^2 = 0$ on the interval $0 < x < 1$ [cite: 8].

Solution:

Differentiate $x^3 + 3xy^2 = 1$ implicitly with respect to $x$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^3) + \frac{d}{dx}(3xy^2) = \frac{d}{dx}(1)$$

Apply the power rule and product rule:

$$3x^2 + \left[ 3 \cdot y^2 + 3x \cdot 2y \frac{dy}{dx} \right] = 0$$
$$3x^2 + 3y^2 + 6xy \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$

Divide the entire equation by 3:

$$x^2 + y^2 + 2xy \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$

Rearranging the terms yields the exact differential equation given [cite: 8]:

$$2xy \frac{dy}{dx} + x^2 + y^2 = 0 \quad \blacksquare$$
