Module 01 • Higher Dimensions

Unit 3.1: The Total Differential

The Intuitive Real-Life Hook: Navigating a Topographical Map

Imagine standing on a hillside. If you walk North, your elevation changes based on the slope in the North direction ($\partial z / \partial y$). If you walk East, your elevation changes based on the Eastward slope ($\partial z / \partial x$). The total differential $dz$ calculates your total approximate change in elevation by combining your movement in both directions simultaneously [cite: 10].

The Mathematical Engine: Definition of Total Differential

If $z = f(x, y)$ and $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$ are increments of $x$ and $y$, then the differentials of the independent variables $x$ and $y$ are $dx = \Delta x$ and $dy = \Delta y$ [cite: 10].

The total differential of the dependent variable $z$ is:

$$dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} dy = f_x(x, y) dx + f_y(x, y) dy$$

This definition can be extended to a function of three or more variables. For instance, if $w = f(x, y, z, u)$, then $dx = \Delta x$, $dy = \Delta y$, $dz = \Delta z$, $du = \Delta u$ [cite: 10]. The total differential of $w$ is:

$$dw = \frac{\partial w}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial w}{\partial y} dy + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} dz + \frac{\partial w}{\partial u} du$$
Faculty Lecture Standard Example 1

Find the total differential for each function [cite: 10]:

  • a. $z = 2x \sin y - 3x^2y^2$
  • b. $w = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$
Model Solution:
a. The total differential $dz$ for $z = 2x \sin y - 3x^2y^2$ is:
$$dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} dy$$

Calculate the partial derivatives:

  • $\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = 2 \sin y - 6xy^2$
  • $\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = 2x \cos y - 6x^2y$
$$dz = (2 \sin y - 6xy^2) dx + (2x \cos y - 6x^2y) dy$$
b. The total differential $dw$ for $w = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ is:
$$dw = \frac{\partial w}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial w}{\partial y} dy + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} dz$$
$$dw = 2x \, dx + 2y \, dy + 2z \, dz$$
Module 02 • Theoretical Foundations

Unit 3.2: Differentiability of Two Variables

Definition of Differentiability

Unlike a single variable function where differentiability simply means a derivative exists, for surfaces $z = f(x, y)$, differentiability requires the surface to be locally "flat" (approximated well by a tangent plane) [cite: 10].

Formal Definition [cite: 10]:

A function $f$ given by $z = f(x, y)$ is differentiable at $(x_0, y_0)$ if $\Delta z$ can be written in the form:

$$\Delta z = f_x(x_0, y_0)\Delta x + f_y(x_0, y_0)\Delta y + \varepsilon_1\Delta x + \varepsilon_2\Delta y$$

where both $\varepsilon_1 \to 0$ and $\varepsilon_2 \to 0$ as $(\Delta x, \Delta y) \to (0, 0)$.

The function $f$ is differentiable in a region $R$ if it is differentiable at each point in $R$ [cite: 10].

Theorem 13.4: Sufficient Condition for Differentiability [cite: 10]

If $f$ is a function of $x$ and $y$, where $f_x$ and $f_y$ are continuous in an open region $R$, then $f$ is differentiable on $R$.

Faculty Lecture Standard Proof Example

Question:

Show that the function given by $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y$ is differentiable at every point in the plane [cite: 10].

Model Proof:

Letting $z = f(x, y)$, the exact increment of $z$ at an arbitrary point $(x, y)$ in the plane is [cite: 10]:

$$\Delta z = f(x + \Delta x, y + \Delta y) - f(x, y)$$
$$= [(x + \Delta x)^2 + 3(y + \Delta y)] - (x^2 + 3y)$$
$$= (x^2 + 2x\Delta x + \Delta x^2) + 3y + 3\Delta y - x^2 - 3y$$
$$= 2x\Delta x + \Delta x^2 + 3\Delta y$$

We now arrange this to match the formal definition of differentiability:

$$= 2x(\Delta x) + 3(\Delta y) + \Delta x(\Delta x) + 0(\Delta y)$$
$$= f_x(x, y)\Delta x + f_y(x, y)\Delta y + \varepsilon_1\Delta x + \varepsilon_2\Delta y$$

where $\varepsilon_1 = \Delta x$ and $\varepsilon_2 = 0$ [cite: 10].

Because $\varepsilon_1 \to 0$ and $\varepsilon_2 \to 0$ as $(\Delta x, \Delta y) \to (0, 0)$, it follows that $f$ is differentiable at every point in the plane [cite: 10]. $\blacksquare$

Module 03 • Approximations

Unit 3.3: Approximation by Differentials & Error Propagation

The exact change in $z$ is $\Delta z$. This change can be approximated by the total differential $dz$ [cite: 10].

$$\Delta z \approx dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} dy$$
Faculty Lecture Standard Approximation Example

Question:

Use the differential $dz$ to approximate the change in $z = \sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}$ as $(x, y)$ moves from the point $(1, 1)$ to the point $(1.01, 0.97)$. Compare this approximation with the exact change in $z$ [cite: 10].

Model Solution:

Letting $(x, y) = (1, 1)$ and $(x + \Delta x, y + \Delta y) = (1.01, 0.97)$ produces:

$dx = \Delta x = 0.01$ and $dy = \Delta y = -0.03$ [cite: 10].

So, the change in $z$ can be approximated by $dz$:

$$\Delta z \approx dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} dy = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}} \Delta x + \frac{-y}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}} \Delta y$$

When $x = 1$ and $y = 1$, you have:

$$\Delta z \approx -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (0.01) - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (-0.03) = \frac{0.02}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} (0.01) \approx 0.0141$$
Comparison with Exact Change:

The exact change corresponds to the difference in heights of two points on the surface of the hemisphere [cite: 10]:

$$\Delta z = f(1.01, 0.97) - f(1, 1)$$
$$= \sqrt{4 - (1.01)^2 - (0.97)^2} - \sqrt{4 - 1^2 - 1^2} \approx 0.0137$$
Faculty Lecture Standard Error Propagation

Question:

The possible error involved in measuring each dimension of a rectangular box is $\pm 0.1$ millimeter. The dimensions of the box are $x = 50$ centimeters, $y = 20$ centimeters, and $z = 15$ centimeters. Use $dV$ to estimate the propagated error in the calculated volume of the box [cite: 10].

Model Solution:

The volume of the box is given by $V = xyz$, and so [cite: 10]:

$$dV = \frac{\partial V}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y} dy + \frac{\partial V}{\partial z} dz$$
$$= yz \, dx + xz \, dy + xy \, dz$$

Using $0.1$ millimeter $= 0.01$ centimeter, you have $dx = dy = dz = \pm 0.01$, and the propagated error is approximately [cite: 10]:

$$dV = (20)(15)(\pm 0.01) + (50)(15)(\pm 0.01) + (50)(20)(\pm 0.01)$$
$$= 300(\pm 0.01) + 750(\pm 0.01) + 1000(\pm 0.01)$$
$$= 2050(\pm 0.01) = \pm 20.5 \text{ cubic centimeters}$$

Practice Problem 1 Total Differential

Find the total differential $dz$ for the function $z = x^2e^{3y}$

Solution:

The formula for the total differential is $dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} dy$.

Calculate $\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}$: Treat $y$ as a constant. The derivative of $x^2$ is $2x$.

$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = 2x e^{3y}$$

Calculate $\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}$: Treat $x$ as a constant. The derivative of $e^{3y}$ is $3e^{3y}$.

$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = x^2 (3e^{3y}) = 3x^2 e^{3y}$$

Substitute these back into the formula:

$$dz = (2x e^{3y}) dx + (3x^2 e^{3y}) dy$$
