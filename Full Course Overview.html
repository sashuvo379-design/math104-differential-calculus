MATH 104: Comprehensive 4-Phase Academic Outline

The DU MATH 104 Differential Calculus syllabus is structured into four progressive learning phases. Use this roadmap to track your mastery from fundamental limits to multivariable optimization and exam-level problem-solving.

PHASE 1 Weight: 30% • Exam Slots 1 & 5

Limits, Continuity & Multivariable Extrema

Foundations of single and multivariable calculus, limit rigorousness, partial derivatives, and optimization on open/closed domains.

Core Topics Covered:
  • Formal $\varepsilon-\delta$ limit proofs for single & multivariable functions.
  • Partial differentiation & Euler's Theorem for homogeneous functions.
  • 3D surfaces, horizontal tangent planes, & Hessian matrix $D(x,y)$.
  • Dr. Md. Rakib Hossain Lecture 15: Slide Examples 3 through 7.
  • Constrained optimization using Lagrange Multipliers ($\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$).
PHASE 2 Weight: 25% • Exam Slot 2

Successive Differentiation & Leibniz Theorem

Higher-order derivatives, nth derivative expressions, Leibniz product rule, and value evaluation at the origin $(y_n)_0$.

Core Topics Covered:
  • Standard $n$-th derivatives of trigonometric, inverse trig, and algebraic forms.
  • Leibniz Theorem for $D^n[u \cdot v]$ expansion.
  • Derivation of non-linear differential equations for $y = (x + \sqrt{1+x^2})^m$.
  • Parity decomposition for odd/even $(y_n)_0$ evaluation.
PHASE 3 Weight: 25% • Exam Slot 3

Mean Value Theorems & Series Expansions

Fundamental theoretical theorems of differential calculus, Taylor and Maclaurin expansions, and indeterminate forms.

Core Topics Covered:
  • Rolle's Theorem, Lagrange's MVT, and Cauchy's Generalized MVT.
  • Trigonometric proofs derived from Cauchy MVT: $\frac{\sin b - \sin a}{\cos b - \cos a} = -\cot c$.
  • Taylor & Maclaurin series with Lagrange and Cauchy forms of remainder.
  • L'Hôpital's Rule for $\frac{0}{0}, \frac{\infty}{\infty}, 0 \cdot \infty, 1^\infty$ indeterminate limits.
PHASE 4 Weight: 20% • Exam Slot 4

Geometrical Invariants, Curvature & Asymptotes

Application of calculus to plane curves, tangent intercept invariants, radius of curvature, and asymptotes of 3rd degree algebraic curves.

Core Topics Covered:
  • Astroid tangent invariant proof ($x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = a^{2/3} \implies \text{Segment } AB = a$).
  • Curvature $\kappa$ and radius of curvature $\rho$ in Cartesian and polar coordinates.
  • Oblique asymptotes of cubic curves ($y = mx + c$) with distinct vs. repeated slope roots ($\phi_3'(m) = 0$).
  • Singular points classification: Nodes, Cusps, and Conjugate Points.
Module 2.0

3D Surface Geometry & Hessian Theoretical Foundations

In multivariable calculus, understanding the physical geometry of surfaces $z = f(x,y)$ connects physical spatial intuition with formal mathematical proofs. At critical points where the gradient vector vanishes $\nabla f(x,y) = (0,0)$, the surface possesses a horizontal tangent plane parallel to the $xy$-plane.

🏔️

Mountain Peak & Basin

At a Local Maximum or Minimum, moving in any direction initially leaves elevation flat. Slope along every axis is zero.

$$\nabla f = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \right) = (0,0)$$
✔ Curvature is negative everywhere for Max, positive for Min.
🐎

The Saddle Point

At a mountain pass (saddle point), moving front-to-back goes downhill, while moving left-to-right goes uphill!

$$f_x = 0, \quad f_y = 0$$ $$\text{Discriminant } D < 0$$
⚠️ Surface curves upwards along one cross-section and downwards along another.
🗺️

Level Curves & Gradient Rays

Topographic maps display level curves $f(x,y) = c$. Gradient vector $\nabla f$ points perpendicular to contours in the steepest ascent direction.

$$\|\nabla f\| = \text{Steepness}, \quad \nabla f \perp \text{Level Curves}$$
💡 Essential for Lagrange Multipliers: $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$.

Why Does the Hessian Discriminant Work? (2D Taylor Expansion Proof)

Let $(a,b)$ be a critical point where $f_x(a,b) = 0$ and $f_y(a,b) = 0$. By multivariable Taylor Expansion:

$$f(a+h, b+k) - f(a,b) = \frac{1}{2!} \left[ f_{xx}(a,b)h^2 + 2f_{xy}(a,b)hk + f_{yy}(a,b)k^2 \right] + R_2(h,k)$$

The sign of local elevation change $\Delta f = f(a+h, b+k) - f(a,b)$ is governed by the quadratic form $Q(h,k) = Ah^2 + 2Bhk + Ck^2$, where $A = f_{xx}, B = f_{xy}, C = f_{yy}$. Multiplying by $A$:

$$A \cdot Q(h,k) = A^2 h^2 + 2AB hk + AC k^2 = (Ah + Bk)^2 + (AC - B^2)k^2$$

Notice the key discriminant term $(AC - B^2) = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2 = D$!

If $D > 0$: Both terms are positive. The sign of $Q(h,k)$ is strictly determined by $A = f_{xx}$.
• $f_{xx} > 0 \implies$ Local Minimum
• $f_{xx} < 0 \implies$ Local Maximum
If $D < 0$: The term $(AC - B^2)$ is negative. $Q(h,k)$ takes both positive and negative values. Indicates a Saddle Point.
If $D = 0$: The quadratic form collapses. Higher-order terms govern surface behavior. The test is Inconclusive.

Slot 1 Limit Proofs via $\varepsilon-\delta$ Definition

High Frequency

Standard DU Question: "Prove by $\varepsilon-\delta$ definition that $\lim_{x \to 2} (x^2 + 3x) = 10$."

Step 1: Target Inequality Formulation:
For any $\varepsilon > 0$, we must find $\delta > 0$ such that if $0 < |x - 2| < \delta$, then $|(x^2 + 3x) - 10| < \varepsilon$.
Step 2: Factor & Bound Auxiliary Term:
$$|(x^2 + 3x) - 10| = |(x - 2)(x + 5)| = |x - 2| \cdot |x + 5|$$ Assume initial bound $\delta_0 = 1$. Then $0 < |x - 2| < 1 \implies 1 < x < 3$.
Adding $5$: $6 < x + 5 < 8 \implies |x + 5| < 8$.
Step 3: Define Final Delta & Formal Conclusion:
$$|(x - 2)(x + 5)| < 8 |x - 2|$$ Set $8 |x - 2| = \varepsilon \implies |x - 2| = \frac{\varepsilon}{8}$.
Therefore, choose $\delta = \min\left(1, \frac{\varepsilon}{8}\right)$. Q.E.D.

Slot 2 Leibniz Recurrence & $(y_n)_0$ Parity Evaluation

Must Solve

Standard DU Question: If $y = \left(x + \sqrt{1+x^2}\right)^m$, find $(y_n)_0$.

1. Derive Differential Equation:
$$y_1 = m\left(x + \sqrt{1+x^2}\right)^{m-1} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right) = \frac{m y}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$$ Squaring both sides: $(1+x^2)y_1^2 = m^2 y^2$. Differentiating again yields: $$(1+x^2)y_2 + x y_1 - m^2 y = 0$$
2. Apply Leibniz's Theorem $n$ times:
$$D^n[(1+x^2)y_2] + D^n[x y_1] - m^2 D^n[y] = 0$$ $$(1+x^2)y_{n+2} + (2n+1)x y_{n+1} + (n^2 - m^2)y_n = 0$$
3. Evaluate at $x = 0$ & Parity Cases:
Setting $x = 0 \implies (y_{n+2})_0 = (m^2 - n^2)(y_n)_0$.
Base values: $y(0) = 1$, $y_1(0) = m$.
• If $n$ is even: $(y_n)_0 = m^2(m^2 - 2^2)(m^2 - 4^2)\dots(m^2 - (n-2)^2)$.
• If $n$ is odd: $(y_n)_0 = m(m^2 - 1^2)(m^2 - 3^2)\dots(m^2 - (n-2)^2)$.

Slot 3 Mean Value Theorems & Trigonometric Proofs

Theoretical Proof

Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem: If $f, g$ are continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$ with $g'(x) \neq 0$, then there exists $c \in (a,b)$ such that:

$$\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{g(b) - g(a)} = \frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)}$$

DU Classic Question Variant: Setting $f(x) = \sin x$ and $g(x) = \cos x$ on $[a,b]$ proves that $\frac{\sin b - \sin a}{\cos b - \cos a} = -\cot c$.

Slot 4 Astroid Tangent Invariant

For astroid $x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = a^{2/3}$, tangent line equation is: $$x \cos\theta + y \sin\theta = a \sin\theta \cos\theta$$ X-intercept $A = (a\cos^3\theta, 0)$, Y-intercept $B = (0, a\sin^3\theta)$.
Length $AB = \sqrt{a^2\cos^6\theta + a^2\sin^6\theta + \dots} = a$ (Constant length)!

Slot 5 Asymptotes of 3rd Degree Cubics

To find oblique asymptotes $y = mx + c$ for cubic $f(x,y) = 0$:
1. Solve $\phi_3(m) = 0$ for slopes.
2. If roots are distinct: $c = -\frac{\phi_2(m)}{\phi_3'(m)}$.
3. If roots are repeated ($m_1 = m_2$): Use quadratic: $$\frac{c^2}{2!}\phi_3''(m) + c\,\phi_2'(m) + \phi_1(m) = 0$$

Top 4 Academic Point-Loss Traps

1. Forgetting Boundary Filtering on Bounded Domains In closed domain extrema problems (e.g. Slide Ex 7), candidates find interior critical points like $(1,1)$ and forget to verify if they lie inside $0 \le x \le 3, 0 \le y \le 2$. Points outside the domain MUST be explicitly discarded!
2. Omitting Continuity & Differentiability Hypotheses In Rolle's or Lagrange's MVT proofs, writing $f'(c) = 0$ without explicitly writing "f(x) is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b)" costs 1.5 out of 5 marks immediately.
3. Division by Zero in Repeated Asymptote Formulas When $\phi_3'(m) = 0$, applying $c = -\frac{\phi_2}{\phi_3'}$ causes division by zero! You MUST switch to the quadratic equation in $c$.
4. Wrong Base Initial Value in Leibniz Recurrence In $(y_n)_0$ problems, candidates mix up $y(0)$ and $y_1(0)$. Even orders $(y_{2k})_0$ start from $y(0)$; Odd orders $(y_{2k+1})_0$ start from $y_1(0)$.
